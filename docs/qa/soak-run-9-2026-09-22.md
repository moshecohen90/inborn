## Method, and the one thing that changed from soak 8

`adb -s REDACTED-6T`, keys only (TAB / DPAD / ENTER / `input text`), every `uiautomator dump` serialised behind one lock
directory, and **one driver process**. Soak 8's cadence is unchanged: F33 every 5 minutes, a `meminfo` /
`thermalservice` / `battery` sample every 5, a new chat every 18, a model switch every 15, a deep-link visit to the
vault, settings and proof every 15, prompt cap 45 polls, and soak 7's completion detector (the stop button seen then
absent twice, with a full-text stability fallback). Two captures ran for the whole window: `logcat -v time
--pid=<app>` and `logcat -b events -v time`.

**What is new: the ledger is read after every answer**, not once per chat window. Soak 8 took four spot readings;
this run takes one per turn, so tok/s, first-token latency and context are a series rather than four points. The
cost is about 45 s a turn, which is why the prompt count is lower than soak 8's — this run trades turns for
per-turn measurement, deliberately.

Two things the ledger rows do **not** cover, both by design. The "What is 2 plus 2?" depth fillers the driver sends
to give an F33 cycle a chat of at least three messages are answers too, and they are not ledgered; they exist to set
up the crash test, not to be measured. And the ledger is read from the **newest** answer only.

## The detector bug this run found in its own first turn

The first attempt at this soak was stopped at minute 2 and thrown away. Its turn 1 printed a ledger card
byte-identical to the previous turn's — 11,521 ms first token, 12.4 s generation — while the send had taken 8 s.
`s-ledger.sh` was TABbing to "the first node whose resource-id is `ledger-toggle`", which on a chat with more than
one answer is an **older** message; every row after that would have been a copy of an earlier answer.

The second attempt, matching the newest toggle by its **bounds**, cannot converge either: the chat list scrolls
under the focus ring, so the target's bounds move between dumps and the equality test never lands. What works is a
single **DPAD_UP from the composer**, which reaches the newest answer's toggle directly because the list is at the
bottom whenever the composer holds focus. The run below is the third attempt, with that fix in place, and its turn 1
reports 1,480 ms / 2.3 s against a 12 s send — a different card from the one before it, which is the point.

### The speed card against the measurement

The lead asked for the card's exact text beside the measured distribution, and for the card's provenance.

**Provenance.** The range is not a per-model constant; it comes from one table, `packages/core/src/catalog/speed.ts`,
keyed by chip class and tier, rendered through `vault.speed` = `"~{min}-{max} tok/s on your {device}"`. The 6T
classifies as **`android-legacy`** (Snapdragon 845, the `OLD_ANDROID_CHIP` branch demotes it from `android-high`
despite its 8 GB), whose row is `{ instant: [12, 18], fast: [5, 7], sharp: [0.4, 0.6] }`. That file is
**byte-identical between `main` 9da93a2 — the source vc16 was built from — and current `origin/main` 7eb123f**
(`git diff` over the path is empty), so the comparison below holds for the build measured *and* for the tip.

| model | turns | min | median | max | mean | the card's exact text on this phone | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FAST** (Qwen3.5 2B) | 4 | 6.1 | **6.4** | 7.0 | 6.5 | **`~5-7 tok/s on your phone`** — photographed, `b-01-vault-top.png`, 16:48 on 1.0.0 (16) | **inside the range, top end reached exactly** |
| **INSTANT** (Qwen3.5 0.8B) | 2 | 14.1 | **14.15** | 14.2 | 14.1 | `~12-18 tok/s on your phone` — **from the table, not photographed** (see below) | **inside the range** |

**Neither card overstates, so there is no F row here.** Fast's whole measured spread sits inside `5-7` and touches
the ceiling at 7.0; Instant's sits inside `12-18`, in its lower half. The estimate on the legacy tier is honest in
the direction that matters — the app does not promise a speed this phone cannot deliver.

**One honesty note on the Instant line.** The Fast card's text is quoted from the screenshot, at full resolution.
The **Instant card's speed line was never photographed** — `b-01-vault-top.png` cuts off two lines above it, and the
vault sweep collected node ids rather than every text, so no capture of this run carries it. `~12-18` above is
derived from `speed.ts` for `android-legacy`, which is what the app must render, not something read off the phone.
It is marked as derived rather than quoted, and re-photographing it needs the 6T, which has gone to `android-vc17`.
The emulator's Instant card *does* read `~6-12`, and that is the **`android-entry`** row for a 4 GB device — a
different card on a different tier, and not this phone's number.

Two things worth naming rather than averaging away. **First-token latency is not stable** — 1.5 s, 4.4 s, 4.7 s,
12.3 s, 15.8 s and 45.6 s, and it tracks the prompt already in context (the 45.6 s turn had 500 tokens in) plus
whatever the model reload after a switch costs, not the answer. And `ms/token` is steady for each model, 142–165 ms
for Fast and 70–71 ms for Instant, across the whole hour: the generation rate itself did not degrade as the phone
stayed busy.

**Memory follows the resident model and does not drift.** Fast sits at **1.71–1.78 GB** and Instant at
**0.93–1.03 GB**, and neither series grows monotonically: Fast's last-but-one sample (min 36, 1.71 GB) is *below*
its first (min 1, 1.73 GB), and Instant's min 51 (0.93 GB) is below its min 31 (1.03 GB). Native heap oscillates
between 259 MB and 403 MB with the same shape. After twelve force-backgrounded cycles, three model swaps and an
hour of generation, the process holds the same footprint it started with — the same conclusion soaks 7 and 8
reached, now with the model switching three times inside one window instead of two windows.

**Two thermal numbers on this phone are not evidence, and are printed here only so nobody mines them later.** The
framework's `Thermal Status:` line reads **6** at every single sample, which in the Android constant table is
`THERMAL_STATUS_SHUTDOWN` — on a phone that ran an hour and never throttled. The hottest `mType=0` CPU sensor reads
**89.9 °C** at eight of nine samples and 89.6 at the ninth, a value that never moves. Both are stuck readings from
this OnePlus 6T's HAL. What does move is the type-3 **skin** sensor and its own `mStatus`, quoted above; it sits at
40.1 °C while Fast is resident and 34.95 °C while Instant is, with **min 46 the one exception** (Fast resident at
34.95), so even that one is a loose correlation and not a law. Soaks 7 and 8 reported a single pinned skin value
(40.29 and 40.116) for their whole windows; this run is the first to see it move, which is what switching models
inside one window buys.

## What this run does not cover

- **Sharp was never resident.** The hour ran Fast and Instant only, as soaks 7 and 8 did.
- **The depth fillers are unledgered.** Nine of the fifteen answers were `What is 2 plus 2?` sent only to give an
  F33 cycle a chat of at least three messages. They were completed and counted, not measured.
- **Fewer turns than soak 8.** 15 sends against soak 8's 26 across comparable time, because a ledger read costs
  about 45 s per turn. This run trades turn count for a per-turn measurement series; the two runs are not
  comparable on prompt count and are comparable on lifecycle, memory and crashes.
- **Nothing was proved about the airplane path, purchases or the vault** here; those are section S of
  `docs/qa/purchases-run-2026-09-11.md`.
