## Method

`adb -s REDACTED-6T`, keys only (TAB / DPAD / ENTER / `input text`), every `uiautomator dump` serialised behind one lock
directory, and **one driver process**. Soak 9's cadence and driver, unchanged: F33 every 5 minutes, a `meminfo` /
`thermalservice` / `battery` sample every 5, a new chat every 18, a model switch every 15 with the chip read back to
confirm it, a deep-link visit to the vault, settings and proof every 15, prompt cap 45 polls, and soak 7's completion
detector (the stop button seen then absent twice, with a full-text stability fallback). Driver in the session scratch
under `android-vc17/drv/`.

cycles. The pid then becomes **932**, and that is **the driver force-stopping the app on purpose**, not a crash: the

revived it (`events.log`: *"focus ring dead — force-stop and relaunch"* 21:40:06 → *"revived, pid=932"* 21:40:25).
The events log agrees: the only `am_kill` for the package in the whole hour is
`stop com.inbornapp.mobile due to from pid 16080` — an `am force-stop` from an adb shell — with no `am_crash`, no
`am_anr` and no `am_proc_died` of its own.

**The crash sweep, and its one honest caveat.** The app-scoped capture (`logcat --pid=`) was armed **before** the soak
started and bound to pid 925, which the driver's first revive replaced with 927 four seconds later, so
`logcat-app.txt` holds 306 lines of a dead process and proves nothing about the hour. The sweep that does is the
device's own main buffer, read after the run: **71,932 lines reaching back to 20.9**, containing **zero**
`FATAL EXCEPTION`, `ANR in` or `SIGSEGV` — none at all, for any package. `dumpsys dropbox` names the app **zero**
times. The events buffer (5,450 lines) carries no `am_crash` or `am_anr` for the app. Next run should arm the
app-scoped capture *after* the first revive, or re-arm it on every pid change.
