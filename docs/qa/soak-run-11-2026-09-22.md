## Method

`adb -s <6t-serial>`, keys only, every `uiautomator dump` serialised behind one lock directory, and **one driver
process**. Soak 10's cadence and driver: F33 every 5 minutes, a `meminfo` / `thermalservice` / `battery` sample every
5, a new chat every 18, a model switch every 15 with the chip read back to confirm it, a deep-link visit to a screen
every 15, prompt cap 45 polls, soak 7's completion detector, and a ledger read after every prompt-bank turn. Driver in
the session scratch under `android-vc18/drv/`.

**One change to the instrumentation, and it is the fix soak 10 asked for.** Soak 10's app-scoped `logcat --pid=`
capture bound to a pid the driver replaced four seconds later and held 306 lines of a dead process, so its crash
claim had to fall back on the device's own buffer. This run therefore armed the **full** buffer as the primary
record, because a full-buffer capture cannot go stale on a pid. That capture was itself cut short at 00:02 when the
shell that started it ended, so the record that actually covers the run is — again — the device's own buffer, read
back after the soak: **56,894 lines reaching from 20.9 21:30 to 23.9 00:25:50**, which is a superset of the window.
The lesson is one layer up from soak 10's: a capture held open by a session process is not a record, whatever it is
scoped to. The next run should have the driver itself own the capture and stop it in its own exit path.

### Fast is slower here than on vc17, and this run cannot say why

Instant's 13.9 tok/s sits inside the `[12, 18]` `android-legacy` row its card is drawn from, the same reading soak 10
took (13.9–14.2). **Fast does not.** Its vault card on this phone promises `~5-7 tok/s`; soak 10 measured 5.7–6.6
across the hour, and this run measured **2.7, 4.8 and 5.1** — the first turn at less than half the card's floor, and
only the third inside the range at all.

What can be said, and what cannot:

- **It is not this diff.** `git diff --name-only 239a268..8033dce` outside `docs/qa/` is seventeen files: the legal
  Markdown, `Legal.tsx` + `legalBody.ts`, the documents/incognito path, the site, `app.config.ts` and the README.
  Nothing in the engine, the catalog, `speed.ts`, the chat path or the prompt path changed between the two builds.
- **The trend inside the run points at warm-up, not at a ceiling.** The three Fast readings *rise* — 2.7 → 4.8 → 5.1
  — while the context they ran at also rose (212 → 436 → 634 tokens), which is the opposite of what a degrading
  device does. Turn 1 began **41 minutes after a 4.8 GB Play update finished installing**, with Play's own background
  work (pack verification, dexopt) still plausible on the machine.
- **Three readings are not a measurement.** Soak 10 needed six to say 5.7–6.6, and §R already recorded the same Fast
  prompt answering at 93 and 52 words fourteen minutes apart. Three points, one of them the first inference after an
  update, cannot separate the build from the machine.

**So this is recorded, not filed.** It is not claimed as a regression, and it is not waved away either: a card that
says `~5-7` was read at 2.7 on this phone, once, and the next soak on a phone that has been idle for an hour is what
would settle it. That soak should take its first Fast reading **after** a warm-up turn, so the number that goes in
the table is not the cold one.
