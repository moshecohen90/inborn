# EU AI Act notes for Inborn

Spec basis: §11.3 (Art. 50 paragraph), §10.5 #36–#38, §11.5. Written 5 September 2026 against the Act's text and the Commission's 2026 guidance. This is an engineering-facing compliance note, not legal advice; the conclusions marked **decision** need Moshe or a lawyer.

## 1. What Inborn is under the Act

| Role in the Act | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Provider of a **general-purpose AI model** (Art. 3(63), obligations in Art. 53–55) | Alibaba/Qwen, Microsoft, Hugging Face, Nomic, OpenAI, Mistral | They trained and released the weights. Their GPAI obligations (documentation, copyright policy, training summary) sit with them. We do not fine-tune, so we do not become a model provider ourselves. |
| **Provider of an AI system** (Art. 3(3)) | **Us.** Inborn is an AI system placed on the EU market under our name. | Art. 50(1) and 50(2) bind the provider of the system, not the model author. Building on a third-party GPAI model does not move the duty: it attaches to whoever ships the final generative system (Commission transparency guidelines, 2026). |
| **Deployer** (Art. 3(4)) | The user | Art. 2(10): the Act does not apply to natural persons using AI in a purely personal, non-professional activity. A lawyer or clinic using Work is a deployer in a professional capacity; their deployer duties (Art. 50(4), Art. 4 literacy) are theirs, and our job is to make them easy (see §5). |
| Distributor of the model weights (Art. 3(7)) | Arguably us, for the bundled Instant model | Weights are redistributed unmodified under Apache-2.0/MIT. No Art. 50 duty arises from distribution; the licence notice duties are covered in `licenses.md`. |

**Risk class:** general-purpose consumer chat is not Annex III high-risk. The Work templates (summarise, draft, redact) do not make the system a decision system in employment, credit, health or justice as long as the app never outputs a decision or score in those areas. Keep it that way: no "should I hire", no triage score, no credit assessment features. If a Work pack ever drifts toward one of those, this classification must be re-done.

**Prohibited practices (Art. 5, in force since 2 February 2025):** nothing in the product touches them (no emotion recognition at work/school, no social scoring, no manipulative techniques, no biometric categorisation). The crisis card (S14) detects wording on-device to show a resource; it does not infer emotional state for any decision, and it must stay that way.

**AI literacy (Art. 4, since 2 February 2025):** applies to providers and deployers for their own staff. For a one-person company this note plus the spec is the record; keep a one-line "read on <date>" log in the repo when a second person joins.

## 2. Article 50: what applies and from when

Article 50 became applicable on **2 August 2026** (Art. 113). The Digital Omnibus package deferred the Annex III high-risk timeline to 2 December 2027, but **left Article 50 in force on schedule**. The only transitional relief is a grace period until **2 December 2026** for the machine-readable *marking* duty of Art. 50(2), and it covers only **generative AI systems already on the market before 2 August 2026**.

**Spec correction.** §11.3 and the gap backlog treat 2.12.2026 as "our" deadline for export stamping. It is not. Inborn will be placed on the market after 2 August 2026, so the grace period does not apply to it: **Art. 50(1) and Art. 50(2) must be satisfied from the first EU release**, whenever that is. The 2.12.2026 date matters to us only as the point after which no competitor can claim the transition either. The "generated by AI" export stamp is therefore a launch requirement (M8), not a December follow-up.

Sources: Commission guidelines on transparency obligations (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations); Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content, finalised 10 June 2026 (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content); Cooley, "EU AI Act: Transparency Obligations Take Effect 2 August 2026"; artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/.

### Art. 50(1): tell people they are talking to AI

Provider duty, applies to Inborn. Users must be informed at first interaction that they are interacting with an AI system, unless obvious to a reasonably well-informed person. The guidelines warn against relying on "obvious", so we do not.

What the app must display (all already in the spec):
- S01 Welcome: one disclosure line, e.g. "Inborn is an AI assistant that runs on this phone. It is software, not a person, and it can be wrong."
- Every persona header carries "AI" and the model name (S-chat header chip, §10.5 #38).
- The first three conversations show the dismissible "can be wrong" notice.
- Shortcuts, widgets and the keyboard extension label output as AI-generated where they display text outside the app.

### Art. 50(2): mark synthetic output in a machine-readable way

Provider duty, applies to Inborn, covers **text** as well as audio, image and video. Exceptions: systems that only assist with standard editing (grammar fixing) or do not substantially alter the input; a "rewrite" quick action might fall under it, a chat answer does not. Marking must be "effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as technically feasible", by state of the art.

The Code of Practice describes a layered approach for providers; for text the practical techniques are metadata and provenance because text watermarking is fragile and not required where infeasible. Inborn's implementation, to be done in the export and share code:

| Surface | Marking |
|---|---|
| Markdown export | YAML front-matter: `ai_generated: true`, `generator: "Inborn <version>"`, `model: "<catalogue id>"`, `generated_at: <ISO date>`; plus a visible first line "Generated with Inborn (on-device AI). Verify before use." |
| PDF export | XMP metadata: `dc:creator` = Inborn, custom `inborn:aiGenerated=true`, `inborn:model`; visible footer line on every page. C2PA content credentials for PDF are the state-of-the-art option once a Rust/JS signer fits the size budget; track as a later improvement, not a launch blocker. |
| JSON export | Top-level `"aiGenerated": true`, `"generator"`, `"model"`, and per-message `"role": "assistant"` already distinguishes AI text. |
| Share sheet / copy | Copied text is the user's clipboard action; the guidelines treat the provider duty as satisfied at the system's output level. Append nothing to the clipboard (users hate it); the in-app label satisfies 50(1) and the export marking satisfies 50(2). |
| Voice output (TTS of AI text) | Audio is generated from AI text; the on-screen label covers the interaction. If we ever export audio files, add ID3/metadata `ai_generated`. |
| Images | Inborn does not generate images at launch. If image generation is added, marking (metadata + watermark) becomes mandatory and non-trivial; treat as a separate compliance item. |

The user's own edits to an exported file are the user's act. Work exports for records (timestamped, §7 Work) keep the marking; the "verify before use" banner text is also the professional's evidence of their own diligence.

### Art. 50(3) and 50(4): not applicable to us

Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation (50(3)): not present. Deployer labelling of deepfakes and public-interest text (50(4)): a deployer duty; private users are out of scope, professional users are told in the Work pack that they must label AI-generated text they publish on matters of public interest.

## 3. What the stores already force that overlaps

Google Play's AI-Generated Content policy (in-app reporting, no prohibited content, developer responsibility) and Apple's age-rating note on chatbot output line up with Art. 50(1) and the safety system prompt. Play's separate "Declaring AI-generated content" checkbox is about **store listing assets** (screenshots, videos), not about the app's runtime output; answer it per asset. Details in `app-privacy-details.md`.

## 4. Penalties and enforcement

Non-compliance with Art. 50 falls under Art. 99(3): fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (SMEs: whichever is lower). Enforcement is by national market-surveillance authorities from 2 August 2026. Signing the Code of Practice is voluntary; signatories get a presumption of conformity for the marking duty, non-signatories must show their own approach is adequate. **Decision for Moshe:** signing the Code is free and possible for small providers; the cost is a public commitment and a yearly report. Recommendation: do not sign at launch (overhead for a one-person shop), keep this note and the export-marking implementation as our adequacy record, revisit at gate C.

## 5. Work tier: professional deployers

Professional users are deployers. What we ship so that they can comply without asking us:
- The printable **architecture statement** (spec §7 Work): what runs where, what leaves the device (nothing), model list with licences, marking behaviour. Never say "HIPAA-compliant", "privileged" or "AI Act-compliant"; say what the architecture does.
- The "verify outputs" banner in Work personas, satisfying the human-oversight expectation in bar guidance (ABA Opinion 512, Florida Bar 24-1, California 2026 guidance).
- Retention controls and timestamped exports so a firm can keep its own records.
- A one-paragraph note in the Work pack: "If you publish AI-generated text on matters of public interest in the EU, Art. 50(4) requires you to disclose that it was AI-generated."
